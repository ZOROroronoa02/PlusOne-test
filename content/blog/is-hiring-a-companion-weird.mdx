Let's name the thing first. You're googling a companionship service, and a small voice is asking whether this makes you sad, desperate, or the kind of person who hires a friend.

Here's the counter-argument, and I'm not going to be delicate about it.

## "Weird" compared to what?

Going to your cousin's wedding alone and spending four hours fielding "so, anyone special?" — that's the widely accepted option. Standing outside a gig because everyone bailed. Eating a work dinner with your eyes on your plate while your team talks about their spouses.

That's the normal. Nobody calls it weird, because it's just what people endure quietly.

Paying ₹499 so that a verified, funny, well-briefed human being walks into the room with you — that's the one people feel strange about.

Notice which of those two situations is actually worse for you.

## The maths of the empty chair

Think about what you'd spend otherwise. One wedding gift. Two cab rides. Four hours of your life doing something you weren't looking forward to. A few drinks to make the small talk bearable. The tax is already being paid — it's just being paid in discomfort instead of rupees.

A plus-one isn't an extra cost. It's swapping one currency for another.

## What people actually book

Not the thing you're half-thinking. The bookings that come in are:

- A wedding in Ghaziabad where the entire extended family has one question.
- An office annual dinner where everyone pairs off and the singles table is a lie.
- A Tuesday gig that no friend will attend.
- A 1am voice call from someone whose brain won't switch off.
- Four hours of a Sunday with a person who will not once check their phone.

Ordinary reasons. Nothing exotic, nothing embarrassing.

## What we refuse to be

PlusOne is platonic. That's not a marketing hedge, it's the operating rule, and it's in writing for both sides before any booking is confirmed. Companions aren't available for anything else, and anybody who pushes gets removed permanently — no refund, no appeal.

We're also not pretending to be therapy. If what you need is clinical help, a companion is the wrong tool, and we say so.

> The service exists because modern life is lonelier than the small talk admits, and because some occasions are simply better with company.

## The only question that matters

Not *"is this weird?"* — but *"will this make the occasion better?"*

For someone who'd otherwise skip the wedding, hide at the dinner, or spend Saturday alone again: yes. Reliably yes.

If you're on the fence, do the cheap version. Book one virtual hour of text or voice — ₹149. No outfit, no travel, no photos. If it's awkward, you've spent less than a pizza and learned something about yourself.

If it isn't, you've found a shortcut through a problem you've been managing badly for years.
