Four weddings between October and February. Three of them say "plus one" on the invite. Your mother has started saying the word *"beta"* in a tone that means something.

You don't need to survive wedding season. You can plan it.

## The four-weddings problem

Most people solve this by skipping two weddings and spending the other two apologising to somebody. That works, but it has a cost: the relatives who notice you always come alone start a file on you, and every year that file gets thicker.

The alternative isn't elaborate. It's just arriving with someone, once or twice, at the weddings that actually matter.

## When a plus-one isn't optional

Book for the ones where your absence would be noticed and your solitude would be discussed:

- **The cousin's wedding.** Full extended family. Highest question-per-hour ratio of the year.
- **The office function.** Not a wedding, but worse — colleagues who'll ask all through the next quarter.
- **The friend's reception.** You're in the photos. Alone in every one of them.
- **The reunion you nearly skipped.** Because the last three, the only thing anyone remembered was that you came by yourself.

Skip the ones where nobody will notice. Book the ones where a chair next to yours decides how the evening goes.

## The briefing sheet

This is the part first-timers get wrong. Your companion can only save you if they know the story. Before the session, tell us:

1. **Who you are in this room.** A cousin, a colleague, a plus-one of a friend.
2. **How you "met".** College? Common friends? A trek you both survived? Keep it boring and repeatable.
3. **The three relatives to avoid.** We'll remove them from the conversational queue.
4. **The question you're dreading.** The classic: "when is your turn?" Companions have a dozen graceful deflections ready.
5. **Who to impress.** There's always one person. Say the name.
6. **Dress code and timings.** Saree-approved or jeans; arrival and exit time.
7. **Your exit strategy.** When the night ends, it ends. No lingering, no follow-up messages unless you ask.

Give us that, and your companion will improvise the other four hours convincingly.

## The relatives question

A companion's job at a wedding is to be pleasant, remember names, and never once be the topic of conversation. Nobody is going to interrogate you at a sangeet while the dhol is playing.

The standard script, if you're ever cornered: *"we met through common friends, still figuring it out."* Vague, polite, and completely closes the topic. Repeatable all season.

## Booking timeline

Wedding season in Delhi NCR runs hot from late October. Weekend evenings — Friday, Saturday, Sunday — sell out first, often a week ahead.

Practical plan:

- **Two to three weeks out:** shortlist the weddings that matter and book the slots.
- **One to two weeks out:** send your companion the briefing sheet.
- **Day before:** confirm timings and meeting point.
- **Two hours before:** your companion confirms they're leaving. You can cancel free of charge up to this point anyway.
